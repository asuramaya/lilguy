# Resources & Search Tactics

## Where postings actually show up
- Company career pages directly (most reliable, no scraping lag): search
  "[company] early careers" or "[company] internship program".
  - Amazon/Walmart/UPS style postings live under their own "early
    careers"/"university recruiting" portals, not the general job board.
  - Handshake (school career center login) surfaces campus-recruited roles
    that don't always appear on public job boards.
- Indeed / Glassdoor / SimplyHired: useful for volume search and to catch
  smaller/regional industrials that don't run a dedicated early-careers site.
- LinkedIn: set a saved search + alert for "supply chain intern",
  "operations intern", "logistics intern" filtered to date-posted.
- Simplify.jobs and similar aggregator lists specifically track internship
  cycles and are updated frequently, good for a periodic sweep but not a
  primary source.

## Professional associations (useful for postings + resume signal)
- ASCM (Association for Supply Chain Management, formerly APICS): student
  membership, job board, and case-competition access.
- CSCMP (Council of Supply Chain Management Professionals): roundtables and
  chapter events are real networking, not just a mailing list.
- ISM (Institute for Supply Management): more procurement-leaning.

## What these programs screen for
- A concrete project or leadership example with a *number* attached
  (reduced X by Y%, managed a team of N, ran a process handling N units/day).
- Excel/data fluency is close to a hard requirement. SQL or a BI tool
  (Power BI/Tableau) is a strong differentiator, not expected by default.
- Case-style interview questions are common at the structured programs
  (PepsiCo, GE, P&G), so practice a basic operations case framework
  (bottleneck identification, inventory tradeoffs, a simple cost/throughput
  calculation).
- Availability for a real 10–12 week commitment, often with a relocation
  stipend. Confirm this doesn't conflict with the school calendar.

## Referral path
Cold applications to these programs go into large pools. A referral from
someone at the company (even a recent alum from the same school) measurably
changes the odds, so check LinkedIn alumni search per target company before
relying on the cold application alone.
